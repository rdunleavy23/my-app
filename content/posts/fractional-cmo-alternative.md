# Why We Don't Offer Fractional CMO Services

## The Problem With Fractional CMOs for Growth-Stage Companies

We get asked constantly if we offer fractional CMO services. The answer is no—and here's why.

The fractional CMO model optimizes for the wrong thing. It's built around ongoing presence when what you actually need is strategic architecture. Fast.

Think about what happens when you hire a fractional CMO:

* 6–12 month minimum commitment
* Monthly retainer of $4k–20k+
* They attend your meetings
* They "advise" on decisions
* Six months in, you still don't have a dashboard
* When they leave, the insights leave with them

> You're renting leadership when you should be building infrastructure.

## What Growth-Stage Companies Actually Need

If you're a $1–5M revenue company and growth has stalled, you don't need someone in your Slack channel. You need five things answered clearly:

**Strategic questions:** Which markets? What positioning? Who's the real ICP?

**Resource questions:** Where does the next dollar go? Build or buy? Right-sized stack?

**Performance questions:** Why isn't it converting? How do we scale without breaking it? What attribution makes decisions easier?

**Org questions:** What stays internal? Who do we hire next? What structure scales?

**Messaging questions:** Is it resonating? What content moves deals? How do we build awareness that matters?

> Get those answered, build a measurement system that shows what drives pipeline, create playbooks your team can execute, train them—then get out of the way.

That's not a 12-month engagement. That's a 2-month sprint.

> The sprint model isn't "fractional CMO lite." It's a different philosophy: you need architecture, not advisory.

## What You Get in 8 Weeks

**Week 1–2: Foundation**
Vision extraction. Competitive map. Real ICP with disqualifiers. Journey clarity.

**Week 3–4: Framework**
Channel model. KPI system. Budget allocation. Campaign templates. Messaging guide.

**Week 5–8: Handoff**
Dashboard connecting your data. Process docs. Team training. Playbooks agencies can run.

**What you own after:**

* Strategy docs your team uses daily
* Dashboard with year of access
* Playbooks anyone can execute
* Measurement showing what drives pipeline
* Complete independence

> No consultant sitting in meetings. No monthly retainer. No wondering what happens when they leave.

## When You'd Actually Want a Fractional CMO

Be honest—there are cases where the traditional model makes sense:

**Choose fractional CMO if:**

* You need day-to-day operational management
* Strategy is clear, execution needs supervision
* You want someone managing politics and vendors
* You have budget for 6–12 months of guidance

**Choose a strategy sprint if:**

* Growth stalled, unclear why
* Team executes well but lacks direction
* You need architecture before headcount
* Board wants predictability you can't show
* You want to own the system, not rent it
* You need answers in weeks, not quarters

> Most growth-stage companies fall into the second category. They think they need a fractional CMO because that's what everyone offers. But what they actually need is the system—fast—then the freedom to run it.

## The Real Cost Comparison

**Fractional CMO (6 months minimum):**

* Cash: $24k–120k+
* Time: 6–12 months to full value
* Risk: Long commitment, unclear deliverables
* Outcome: Consultant-dependent insights

**Strategy Sprint:**

* Cash: Fixed project fee (know upfront)
* Time: 8 weeks to complete handoff
* Risk: Contained—you know exactly what you're getting
* Outcome: You own everything, no dependency

> The question isn't which costs less. It's which gives you strategic clarity faster—with nothing held hostage when it's done.

## You Probably Need This If

* Revenue $1–5M, growth slowing despite decent execution
* Board asking questions you can't confidently answer
* Data scattered, no unified view of what drives pipeline
* Ambitious targets, low confidence current approach scales
* Full-time CMO economics don't fit but you need CMO-level thinking

> Most companies in this position don't need 12 months of advisory. They need 8 weeks of architecture.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why don't you offer traditional fractional CMO services?

Because the model optimizes for the wrong outcome. Fractional CMOs create ongoing dependency through monthly retainers and fuzzy deliverables. Growth-stage companies need strategic architecture they own—clear positioning, working measurement systems, executable playbooks—then the freedom to run it. That's an 8-week sprint, not a 12-month advisory relationship.

### What's wrong with fractional CMO engagements?

Nothing, if you need day-to-day operational management. But most growth-stage companies don't—they need answers to strategic questions, a measurement system, and playbooks their team can execute independently. Fractional models charge $4k–20k+/month for ongoing presence when what you actually need is architecture delivered fast with complete handoff.

### How is a strategy sprint different from hiring a fractional CMO?

Fractional CMOs provide ongoing advisory on monthly retainers (typically 6–12+ months). Strategy sprints deliver complete strategic architecture in 8 weeks as a fixed-scope project. You get the insights and systems faster, own everything permanently, and have zero ongoing dependency or consulting fees.

### What happens when the sprint ends?

You own everything: strategy documents, competitive frameworks, messaging guides, campaign playbooks, custom dashboard (with year of access), measurement models, and trained team. No consultant dependency, no recurring fees. The architecture is yours to use indefinitely. That's the point—you wanted a system, not a relationship.

### When would I actually want a fractional CMO instead?

Choose a fractional CMO if you need someone managing day-to-day operations, vendor relationships, team politics, and tactical execution for an extended period. Choose a sprint if you need strategic clarity fast—positioning, measurement, playbooks—then want your team to execute independently without ongoing consulting fees.
